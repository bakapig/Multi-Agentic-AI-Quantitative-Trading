1 THE INVESTMENT PROPOSITION
SHOW: Overview, then the architecture diagram.

SAY:
Good afternoon. We are presenting Meridian, a systematic equity research fund
that combines multiple trading signals within an independently controlled
portfolio. Our objective is to improve the consistency of risk-adjusted returns
relative to a declared equity benchmark, using an allocation process that an
investor can inspect from signal to approval.

Our research hypothesis is that different price patterns can be useful in
different conditions. Trend following seeks persistence in an asset's direction.
Cross-sectional momentum seeks relative strength between assets. Mean reversion
seeks convergence in a relative-price spread. We express those hypotheses as
separate engines, then make the capital allocation explicit.

The illustrative mandate is five million US dollars: three million assigned
to the ensemble and two million in central cash. We are demonstrating the
investment-decision and control architecture. Our proposal is a staged research
mandate, with further allocation conditional on reconciled forward performance
and execution evidence.

2 THE INVESTABLE UNIVERSE AND INFORMATION SET
SHOW: Ensemble & strategies, with the Historical replay label visible.

SAY:
The connected fund adapter evaluates fourteen US equities in USD, using SPY
as its research benchmark and XNYS exchange sessions. Keeping one currency and

Every proposal uses information through its stated signal session. Future rows

3 THE THREE RETURN HYPOTHESES
SHOW: Strategy cards and saved engine target weights.

SAY:
Our trend engine is long-or-cash. An asset qualifies when its ten-day moving
average exceeds its sixty-day average, and SPY must pass the same condition.
The engine equally weights qualifying names. Its hypothesis is directional
persistence; its vulnerability is repeated reversal around the signal.

Our momentum engine ranks sixty-day returns and rebalances on a ten-session
schedule. With sector data, eligible sectors receive equal budgets. Within
each sector, we buy stronger names and short weaker names, scaling positions
inversely with trailing twenty-session return volatility.

That targets dollar balance within the selected sectors. It does not estimate
or eliminate market beta. Stock-specific, factor and financing exposures remain.

Our mean-reversion engine uses predefined equity pairs. It subtracts a rolling
twenty-session mean from the log-price difference and standardises the result.
The connected policy enters a flat pair at an absolute Z-score of at least two,
uses a zero-point-two convergence exit band, and applies a three-point-five
adverse-divergence exit threshold to held positions.

These are candidate pairs. Economic similarity and a rolling Z-score do not
prove a stationary spread. This engine does not reuse the retired Kalman and
OU formation tests. Econometric qualification of the current pair set is part
of the research case before approving a funded mean-reversion allocation.

TECHNICAL DISPLAY:
  Trend eligibility: MA10(P_i,t) > MA60(P_i,t), with the same SPY filter.
  Momentum score:   M_i,t = P_i,t / P_i,t-60 - 1.
  Position scaling within a selected side: w_i,t proportional to 1 / sigma_i,t.
  Pair spread:      s_AB,t = log(P_A,t) - log(P_B,t).
  Reversion score:  z_AB,t = (s_AB,t - mean20(s_AB)) / std20(s_AB).

4 CAPITAL ALLOCATION AND THE REGIME ROUTER
SHOW: Market regime and strategy capital weights.

SAY:
The regime detector combines recent SPY return, annualised twenty-session
volatility, and a directional-strength proxy calculated from closing prices.
It is a transparent threshold classifier, with no calibrated regime probabilities.

Volatility of at least thirty percent takes priority as the crisis state.
A directional-strength reading of at least twenty-five defines trending
conditions. Low directional strength and volatility define ranging conditions;
other observations enter transition.

This snapshot is classified as ranging. Its policy assigns ten percent of the
ensemble budget to trend, eighty percent to momentum, and ten percent to cash.
Mean reversion receives zero. Three engines are implemented, while the current
regime policy funds two. The zero weight is an existing policy setting, not
evidence that we have established an optimal allocation.

Equal-weight and inverse-volatility alternatives can fund all three engines.
Inverse volatility uses observed strategy returns through the signal date to
size the next proposal. It is an allocation heuristic, not a full covariance-
based equal-risk-contribution optimisation.

We aggregate shared stock symbols before proposing the portfolio. A stock's
dollar target equals the ensemble budget multiplied by the weighted sum of
engine targets. Dividing by its price gives the proposed units. Those units
are intended for a later eligible session, not a fill at the signal close.

TECHNICAL DISPLAY:
  Dollar target D_i,t = B_t * sum_s(a_s,t * w_s,i,t).
  Unit target   q_i,t = D_i,t / P_i,t.
  B_t is capped by confirmed sleeve NAV and its capital mandate.
  a_s,t is strategy allocation; w_s,i,t is an engine's stock weight.
  Current a = (trend 0.10, momentum 0.80, reversion 0.00, cash 0.10).

5 THE PROPOSED PORTFOLIO
SHOW: Overview and approved targets.

SAY:
The supplied account contains five million dollars of cash and no confirmed
holdings. The portfolio proposes two point seven million dollars of gross
exposure, or fifty-four percent of fund NAV. Net exposure is three hundred
thousand dollars, or six percent of NAV.

Gross measures the absolute size of long and short positions. Net measures
their signed dollar imbalance. Six percent net is not a beta estimate, and
fifty-four percent gross is not a volatility forecast.

The distinction between the cash account and proposed positions is deliberate.
This integration records intentions. It does not turn targets into holdings
or claim a trade has filled. These are portfolio-construction measurements,
not investment returns.

TECHNICAL DISPLAY:
  Gross exposure = sum_i(abs(q_i * P_i)) / NAV = 54%.
  Net exposure   = sum_i(q_i * P_i) / NAV      = 6%.
  Connected integration's realised fills      = 0.

6 INDEPENDENT RISK CONTROL
SHOW: Risk & protection, guardrails, then the Phantom Book.

SAY:
The investment engines propose; an independent controller decides whether the
proposal fits the mandate. Under standard policy, gross cannot exceed eighty
percent of fund NAV, and any symbol cannot exceed twenty percent. Collateral
and fee reserves can impose tighter constraints first.

At a twelve-percent observed drawdown, the standard gross ceiling falls from
eighty to forty percent of NAV. At twenty-five percent, the persistent breaker
requests exits and remains latched across restarts. These are control responses,
not guarantees that realised losses cannot exceed those thresholds.

Risk applies a common scale to the proposed portfolio, preserving its relative
proportions within the binding limits. In this snapshot, the largest symbol is
approximately seven point two percent of NAV, and no reduction is required.

The Phantom Book compares proposed and approved gross exposure. The difference
here is zero. A positive difference measures exposure removed. Calling it
profit saved would require a separate counterfactual return calculation.

7 TWO LANES AND THE ROLE OF AI
SHOW: System flow, then Committee review.

SAY:
The quantitative lane contains validated prices, strategy targets, allocation,
independent risk and saved intentions. The advisory lane reviews the evidence
after risk has decided. Humans own the strategy and risk policy.

The ensemble analyst inspects signals, model health and positions. The Committee
Chair reviews exposure, the Phantom Book and the analyst memo. Neither role can
change portfolio weights, override risk or place an order.

Default reviews use deterministic tools. Automatic LLM provider calls are
pending. Optional imported commentary must refer to the exact facts digest,
and its structured metric claims are checked against those facts. That does
not validate every narrative statement.

The investor benefit we are building toward is an explanation tied to the
investment record. Plausible prose does not become a signal or an approval.

TECHNICAL INVESTOR AND EXAMINER QUESTIONS

1 WHERE DOES YOUR EDGE COME FROM?
Our hypotheses are directional persistence, cross-sectional relative strength,
and convergence in selected spreads. The implementation expresses these as
transparent rules. Edge requires net out-of-sample evidence; architecture alone
does not establish it.

2 IS THIS REALLY A THREE-STRATEGY PORTFOLIO?
Three engines are implemented and run for diagnostics. The current regime
policy gives mean reversion zero in every state. Equal-weight and inverse-
volatility alternatives can fund it. Describe the active policy accurately.

3 WHY DOES RANGING GIVE MOMENTUM EIGHTY PERCENT?
That is the teammate's existing fixed allocation map. Do not describe it as an
econometrically optimal answer. It needs development-sample justification and
comparison with simple baselines, followed by a frozen evaluation.

4 ARE THE PAIRS COINTEGRATED?
The connected mean-reversion engine does not test cointegration. Economic
similarity and a rolling Z-score do not establish a stationary spread. Test
the intended pair universe on a formation sample before funding that sleeve.
Account for repeated pair searches and structural breaks in the research.

5 DOES YOUR TREND ENGINE USE ADX AND ATR STOPS?
The connected vectorized engine uses moving-average and benchmark filters.
Its ADX and ATR constructor parameters do not implement those controls in the
current signal path. Do not claim active ADX entry filtering or an ATR trailing
stop for this engine. The regime router separately uses a close-based
directional-strength proxy, rather than full high-low-close ADX.

6 IS MOMENTUM MARKET NEUTRAL?
The sector-data path targets equal long and short dollar budgets within
eligible sectors, with inverse-volatility sizing on each side. That does not
guarantee zero market beta or zero factor exposure. Estimate those exposures
separately from net dollar exposure.

7 IS INVERSE VOLATILITY THE SAME AS RISK PARITY?
The option is labelled risk_parity in configuration. Its rule normalises inverse
standalone strategy volatility. It does not solve a full covariance-based
equal-risk-contribution optimisation.

8 HAVE YOU ELIMINATED LOOK-AHEAD BIAS?
The adapter truncates future input rows and proposes targets for a later
session. That is a verified boundary. It does not prove the entire research
dataset, universe selection, parameter choice or standalone backtest is free
of leakage. Those need separate, dated validation.

9 WHAT ARE YOUR ACTUAL SHARPE RATIO AND MAXIMUM DRAWDOWN?
The connected fund has no reconciled return history, so its achieved Sharpe
and historical maximum drawdown are unavailable. Current snapshot drawdown
is not backtest maximum drawdown. Use a costed return ledger before presenting
fund performance figures.

10 HOW ARE COSTS HANDLED?
Fund risk reserves a fifteen-basis-point fee buffer and a collateral buffer.
It does not book fees, borrow charges or fills. Standalone research has its
own assumptions. A reserve is not a realised cost model; investment results
need consistent execution and financing accounting.

11 DOES THE BREAKER CAP LOSSES AT TWENTY-FIVE PERCENT?
No. It reacts to observed drawdown by requesting exits and persisting its
latched state. Gaps, delayed observations and execution failures can cause
realised losses beyond a threshold.

12 WHAT DOES THE AI ADD TODAY?
Read-only evidence tools and a boundary for importing commentary linked to
computed facts. No automatic LLM call or AI-generated trading decision is
claimed. Faster inspection is the intended advisory benefit, which also needs
evaluation when a provider is connected.

13 IS YOUR AUDIT LOG TAMPER-PROOF?
It has transactions, append-only database triggers and content hashes inside
a trusted local process. This is not external attestation or protection from
an administrator changing the database and application.

14 WHAT IS NEEDED BEFORE REAL INVESTOR CAPITAL?
A reconciled account and costed forward return history; executable market data,
lot and borrow checks; reliable order/fill processing; tested operational
controls; and separately approved hosting, access and account arrangements.
The current system supports a research-stage mandate.
